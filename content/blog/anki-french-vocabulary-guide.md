## Introduction

Your level in French depends far more on how many words you know than on how many grammar rules you've memorized. Grammar matters, of course, but vocabulary is what makes communication possible. Imagine trying to say "I eat a banana" in French: if you know the words but not the grammar, you'll still be understood. If you know the grammar perfectly but not the vocabulary, you won't communicate anything at all.

That's why I recommend **Anki** to almost every student I teach. It's a free flashcard app built specifically around a memory technique that works — and in this guide, I'll walk you through exactly how to set it up and use it well.

## What Anki Actually Does

Anki uses a **Spaced Repetition System (SRS)**. Think of memory as having two layers: short-term and long-term. The goal of vocabulary learning is to push information from the short-term layer into the long-term one, and the way you do that is by reviewing information at increasing intervals.

Here's how it works in practice. Each flashcard has a front and a back — for French, that typically means a French word or sentence on the front and its translation on the back. When you review a card, Anki asks: do you remember this?

- If you answer **yes**, the card comes back in a few days.
- If you answer **no**, it comes back the next day.

The better you know a card, the further apart its reviews get spread. This ensures information keeps resurfacing right before you're about to forget it — which is exactly what pushes it into long-term memory.

## Getting Started

1. Go to the official Anki website and download the version for your operating system (the desktop app is free on Windows, Mac, and Linux).
2. Install the software and choose your interface language.
3. Create a new deck — call it something like "French Vocabulary."
4. Click **Add** to create your first card, filling in the French term on the front and the translation on the back.
5. Use the **Browse** menu to view, edit, or reorganize all the cards you've created.
6. Check the **Stats** section regularly — seeing your progress visually is genuinely motivating.

One important note for mobile users: the official Anki app costs around 25€ on iOS but is free on Android. Be careful when searching the App Store — there are unofficial apps also called "Anki" that won't sync with your desktop account.

## How to Build Effective Cards

This is where most learners go wrong. A few rules I give my students:

- **Use full sentences, not isolated words.** A sentence gives you context and lets you absorb sentence structure at the same time. "Le midi, je mange une pizza" teaches you far more than "pizza" alone.
- **One unknown word per card.** If a sentence has too many words you don't know yet, the card becomes inefficient — your brain has nothing familiar to anchor the new information to. Beginners can stretch to two or three unknown words, but should move toward one as soon as possible.
- **Personalize your sentences.** A sentence generated by ChatGPT about a stranger's day is forgettable. A sentence about your actual lunch, your actual friends, or your actual routine sticks far better — because you have an emotional or personal connection to it.
- **Add images when you can.** We remember visual information more easily than text alone. If you're learning "un restaurant," attach a photo of your favorite one.

## Pre-Made Decks vs. Your Own Cards

You can download ready-made decks from the Anki website containing the most frequently used French words — useful if you're short on time, since these words are statistically the ones you'll encounter most often. But in my experience, self-made cards are almost always more effective, because the act of creating a card (choosing the sentence, thinking about the word) is itself part of the memorization process. A hybrid approach works well: use a pre-made deck as a base, but personalize the sentences over time.

## Build a Habit, Not a Sprint

The single most important factor in vocabulary learning isn't the app — it's consistency. A well-known trick from *Atomic Habits* by James Clear is to attach a new habit to one you already have. If you drink coffee every morning, decide that reviewing your Anki cards happens right after that first cup.

Practical guidelines:

- Start with **5 new cards per day**, and only increase after one or two weeks.
- **10 new cards per day** is a comfortable long-term pace for most learners; 15 starts to demand real time investment.
- If you skip a day, your review pile will grow. When that happens, set new cards to zero and clear your backlog before adding anything new.
- Watch your ratio of Anki time to immersion time. If flashcards start eating into the time you'd otherwise spend listening to or reading French, scale back — immersion should always come first.
- Avoid reviewing on autopilot. Distracted, half-focused review sessions are far less effective than five genuinely concentrated minutes.

## Conclusion

Anki isn't magic — it's a tool that makes a proven memory principle easy to apply daily. Used well (personalized sentences, one unknown word per card, a sustainable daily habit), it becomes one of the most efficient ways to build real, lasting French vocabulary. Used badly (huge decks of impersonal AI-generated sentences, reviewed on autopilot), it becomes just another app you eventually abandon. The difference isn't the software — it's how you use it.
